title: Readable emails, safer email display, fuller replies and faster bulk actions
---

Fixes for the rest of our audit (emails that showed blank or garbled, a stronger wall around email content, quicker bulk actions) and a few things you asked for.

- **Replies and forwards keep the original's images and tables** in the quoted email, instead of plain text.
- **See where a link goes before you click it.** Hover a link in an email and its address shows at the bottom of the window.
- **Scheduled emails show what's happening.** The Scheduled list says when one is sending, or why it couldn't be sent yet (Shift keeps trying).
- **Settings in the menu bar.** Shift → Settings… (⌘,).

- **Emails that showed blank or garbled now display.** Long emails Gmail stores separately are fetched, and emails in other character sets (common outside English) are decoded properly, including ones already on your Mac.
- **Emails are fully walled off from Shift.** Each email now runs separately from the app, so nothing in an email can reach your mail or settings.
- **Bulk actions are faster.** Archiving, labelling and Clean up send one request per account instead of one per email, and you can select up to 250 emails at once.
- **Editing a scheduled email keeps it scheduled.** It pauses while you edit and goes out at the same time when you close it, or when you quit. The time picker now explains that Shift sends from your Mac, so it needs to be running.
- **Opening an attachment again** reuses the copy already in Downloads instead of saving another.
- **Undo after Delete puts mail back where it was**, not in the Inbox, even right after a big bulk delete.
- **Fewer interruptions.** A sync error shows once, not every 30 seconds, and never covers Undo or Restart.
- **Signing out warns about scheduled emails** that won't be sent.
- **One damaged account no longer stops Shift opening.** The other accounts open, and Shift explains how to add it back.
- **Settings changes aren't lost** when you quit straight after making them.
- Sent emails no longer include your Mac's name in a hidden header, and AI writing help is harder to trick with text inside an email.
